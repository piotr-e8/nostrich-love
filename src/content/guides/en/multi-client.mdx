## Why Multiple Clients? (2 minutes)

### The Power of Choice

Nostr's superpower is that your identity works everywhere. Smart users take advantage of this by using multiple clients for different purposes.

### Common Use Cases

**Mobile + Desktop:**

- **Phone (Damus/Amethyst):** Quick checks, on-the-go posting, notifications
- **Desktop (Iris/Snort):** Longer posts and threads, heavy browsing, work hours

Full articles are a different job. Iris and Snort compose short notes, so if you
write essays, use a client built for long-form posts. [YakiHonne](https://yakihonne.com)
is the usual choice and runs in the browser as well as on iOS and Android.

**Two separate identities:**

- Some people keep a second keypair for a personal account and give each keypair
  its own client
- The separation comes from the two sets of keys, not from the app. One keypair
  used in two clients is still one public identity, with the same npub and the
  same public posts everywhere

**Different features:**

A client can earn its place by doing one thing well: Amethyst for its relay and
media controls, 0xchat for private messages, Coracle for browsing individual
relays. Everything else about your account behaves the same in all of them.

**Backup & Redundancy:**

- If one client breaks, you have alternatives
- Test new features safely
- Compare interfaces

### Benefits

The gain is that you stop bending your habits to fit one app. Write where writing
is comfortable, read where reading is comfortable, and keep the two contexts
apart on purpose instead of by accident. Little is at stake in a switch, because
your identity, posts and follows live on relays rather than inside any client.

<Callout variant="info">
  **You don't need multiple clients.** But if you're serious about Nostr, it
  unlocks new workflows.
</Callout>

## Syncing Strategy (3 minutes)

### What Syncs Automatically

**Universal (works across all clients):**

- ✓ Your identity (npub)
- ✓ Posts you publish
- ✓ Follows list
- ✓ Profile information
- ✓ Reactions (likes, reposts)

DMs are the exception. Private messages show up in a second client only if that
client speaks the same DM standard (the modern one is NIP-17) and is connected to
your DM relays. A client still on the older standard shows you a different,
separate conversation history. See
[private messages](/guides/nip17-private-messages) for which clients are where.

### What Doesn't Sync

**Client-specific:**

- ✗ App settings (themes, fonts)
- ✗ Notification preferences
- ✗ App-local blocks and per-app hides (your standard mute list does sync)
- ✗ Draft posts
- ✗ Local bookmarks
- ✗ Custom feeds/algorithms
- ✗ App-specific data

### The Syncing Process

**How it works:**

1. You post from Client A
2. Client A sends to relays
3. Client B connects to same relays
4. Client B sees your post
5. Your posts and follows stay in sync

That third step is the whole trick. Both clients look for your posts on the
relays in your published relay list, so if one client is pointed somewhere your
posts never went, it will show you a thinner version of your own account. Keep
the same relay list in every client you use. The
[outbox model](/guides/outbox-model) explains how clients find that list.

**Time delay:**

- Usually instant to a few seconds
- Depends on relay speed
- Client refresh rate varies

### Workarounds for Non-Syncing Data

**Settings:**

- Manually configure each client
- Document your preferences
- Use similar settings for consistency

**Mutes/Blocks:**

- Your mute list is part of the protocol (NIP-51), so it travels with your
  account: mute someone in one client and the others pick it up
- What stays behind is app-local stuff, a per-app "hide this" toggle or a
  client's own filter settings
- No export or import needed

**Drafts:**

- Use external notes app
- Copy-paste between clients
- Or just post immediately

**Bookmarks:**

- Use NIP-51 lists (if supported)
- External bookmarking tools
- Nostr-based bookmarking services

<Callout variant="success">
  **Pro tip:** Focus on what syncs (content, follows) and don't worry too much
  about what doesn't (settings).
</Callout>

## Desktop + Mobile (3 minutes)

### Recommended Pairings

**iOS User:**

- **Mobile:** Damus (clean, fast)
- **Desktop:** Iris (web) or Snort (web)
- **Why:** Damus is iOS-optimized, web clients work everywhere

**Android User:**

- **Mobile:** Amethyst (feature-rich)
- **Desktop:** Iris (web) or Coracle (web)
- **Why:** Amethyst is Android's best, pair with fast web clients

**Cross-Platform:**

- **Mobile:** Primal (iOS + Android)
- **Desktop:** Same Primal account (web)
- **Why:** Unified experience across devices

### Workflow Examples

**Content Creator:**

- **Draft on desktop** (Iris) - Write, edit, format
- **Post from desktop** - Hit publish
- **Check engagement on mobile** (Damus) - Replies, zaps
- **Quick replies on mobile** - While out and about

**Community Manager:**

- **Monitor on mobile** (Amethyst) - Notifications, alerts
- **Deep engagement on desktop** (Snort) - Long replies, threads
- **DMs on mobile** - Quick conversations
- **Event planning on desktop** - Detailed posts

**Casual User:**

- **Browse on mobile** (any) - During downtime
- **Post from either** - Whatever's convenient
- **No strict workflow** - Just use what feels right

### Keeping Track

**Notifications:**

- Enable on mobile for urgent stuff
- Disable on desktop if distracting
- Or enable both, mute strategically

**Read State:**

- Nostr doesn't track "read" status
- Your client does locally
- May see same posts as "new" on different clients
- This is normal, not a bug

## Client-Specific Features (2 minutes)

### Unique Features by Client

**Damus (iOS):**

- Best iOS experience
- Smooth animations
- Great notifications
- Apple ecosystem integration

**Amethyst (Android):**

- Most customizable
- Advanced relay controls
- Great for power users
- Feature-rich

**Iris (Web):**

- Works everywhere
- Fast and minimal
- Good for desktop
- Simple interface

**Primal (All platforms):**

- Beautiful design
- Easy discovery
- Unified experience
- Beginner-friendly

**Snort (Web):**

- Keyboard shortcuts
- Fast performance
- Developer-friendly
- Minimal UI

**Coracle (Web):**

- Relay-first: pick relays and browse them individually
- Moderation built on your web of trust
- Communities, calendar events and classifieds
- Multiple accounts in one browser

### Choosing Based on Task

Desktop wins for anything you have to type at length; the phone wins for reading
and for the short reply you fire off while away from a desk. Media and private
messages vary more by client than by device, so if photos are central to what you
publish, or you want the modern private messages, check that a client handles
them properly before you make it your main one.

<Callout variant="success">
  **Experiment:** Try different clients for different tasks. You'll quickly find
  what works best for you.
</Callout>

## Backup & Migration (2 minutes)

### Moving Between Clients

Adding a client is easy. How you sign in to it is the part worth getting right,
because your nsec cannot be rotated or revoked: every app you paste it into keeps
it forever.

1. Install the new client
2. Sign in the way that fits the platform (below)
3. Wait for it to pull your profile and follows
4. Start using it

**Web clients (Iris, Snort, Coracle, Primal web):** connect a signer extension,
[Alby](https://getalby.com) or [nos2x](https://github.com/fiatjaf/nos2x), or a
remote signer such as [nsec.app](https://nsec.app). The site asks the signer to
approve each action and never sees your key. Do not paste your nsec into a
website, no matter how well known it is.

**Android:** use [Amber](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber). The client hands
each signing request to Amber, you approve it there, and your nsec never enters
the client itself. Amber is not on Google Play; get it from F-Droid, the Zap
Store or its GitHub releases page.

**iOS:** an App Store app is sandboxed, so pasting your nsec into a client you
trust is the common route. If you would rather not hand it over at all, Primal
can approve activity in other Nostr apps through its Remote Login feature, and
[Nostash](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nostash/id6744309333) is a signer
extension for Safari on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

<Callout variant="warning">
  This is the same rule as in the [quickstart](/guides/quickstart): a website
  that asks you to type your private key into a form is a red flag, and the fix
  is a signer.
  [Privacy and security](/guides/privacy-security) walks through setting one up.
</Callout>

**What transfers:**

- Identity
- Follows list
- Profile info
- Published posts

**What doesn't:**

- Settings
- Drafts
- Local data

### Export and Import

There is less to export than you would expect. Your follows and your mute list
are published to relays as lists signed by you, so a new client picks them up on
its own once it connects to the right relays, and no file changes hands.
Bookmarks come along the same way in clients that keep them as a Nostr list
instead of on the device. What stays behind is per-app configuration, and only
some clients offer any way to save that.

### Testing New Clients

**Safe approach:**

1. Create a throwaway keypair first
2. Try the client with that test account
3. If you like it, connect your real identity through your signer
4. Keep the old client as a backup

**Don't:**

- Paste your nsec into a website, ever
- Test with your main identity
- Rush the migration

## Advanced Workflows [ADVANCED]

### Professional Setup

**Content Creator:**

- **Planning:** whatever notes app you already use
- **Writing:** desktop client, full screen
- **Engagement:** mobile through the day
- **Numbers:** zap and reach stats live in separate tools, see
  [Nostr tools worth knowing](/guides/nostr-tools)

**Community Manager:**

- **Monitoring:** mobile, always on
- **Response:** desktop for the long replies
- **Events:** a client with calendar support, such as Coracle
- **Numbers:** the same tools as above

Cross-posting bridges and scheduling bots exist, and they are also the fastest
way to make an account read like a feed instead of a person. Use them sparingly.

## Troubleshooting Multi-Client Issues

### Out of Sync

**Posts not showing:**

- Different relay connections
- Wait a few minutes
- Pull to refresh
- Check relay status

**Follows different:**

- One client not fully synced
- Different relay sets
- Wait and refresh

### Follows Vanished After Opening an Old Client

A client you have not touched in months can still be holding a months-old copy of
your follow list. If you follow or unfollow someone before it finishes catching
up, it publishes that old copy over your current one and the people you added
since are gone. Open it, let it load, check that the follow count looks right,
and only then follow anyone.

### Notification Overload

**Too many alerts:**

- Disable on less-used clients
- Keep only mobile notifications
- Use Do Not Disturb strategically

### Confusion

**Lost track:**

- Pick primary and secondary
- Use consistent usernames
- Document your setup
