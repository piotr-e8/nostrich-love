### You're Ready!

Open your chosen client and log in. **How** you log in matters more than most
guides admit.

**On Android**, install [Amber](https://github.com/greenart7c3/Amber) and let it
hold your key. Apps ask Amber to sign, you approve each request there, and your
nsec never enters the app itself. Amber is not on Google Play, so get it from
zap.store, F-Droid or its GitHub releases page.

**On iPhone**, there is no equivalent, so paste your nsec into the app once. What
you store inside an app stays inside that app, but the clipboard is shared
ground: while your key sits there, other apps can read it, and a keyboard that
syncs your clipboard to the cloud can carry it off the phone. Turn that sync off
before you paste, and copy something harmless over the key as soon as you are
logged in.

**In a web browser, do not paste your nsec into the website.** Install a signer
extension instead: [Alby](https://getalby.com),
[nos2x](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/nos2x/kpgefcfmnafjgpblomihpgmejjdanjjp)
or [Nostash](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/nostash/id6744309333) on Safari. Your
key lives in the extension; the website asks the extension to sign each action
and never sees the key itself. Every serious web client supports this (it is
called NIP-07), and it turns "one bad website costs you your identity" into "one
bad website gets nothing."

If you would rather keep your key off your devices entirely, a remote signer such
as [nsec.app](https://use.nsec.app) (NIP-46) holds it and approves actions from a
separate app.

<Callout variant="warning" title="A website that asks for your nsec is a red flag">
  Once a site has your nsec, it has your identity permanently. You cannot rotate
  a Nostr key or revoke access. There is no password reset and no support desk.

Legitimate web clients ask you to connect a signer. If a site asks you to type or
paste your private key directly into a form, close the tab.

</Callout>
