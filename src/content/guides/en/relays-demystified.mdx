Relays are the post offices of Nostr. They're where your messages live and how they travel to reach other users.

## The Post Office Analogy

Imagine Nostr as a network of post offices around the world.

- **You** have a mailbox (your keys)
- **Relays** are post offices
- **Posts** are letters you send

Here's the key difference from traditional social media: **You choose where to send your mail.**

### The Power of Choice

In Nostr, you're not locked into one service provider. You can:

- Send posts to any relay
- Connect to multiple relays
- Switch relays anytime
- Run your own relay

## Choosing Relays

### Default Recommendations

If you're just starting, stick with these popular, reliable relays:

| Relay                    | Description             | Cost |
| ------------------------ | ----------------------- | ---- |
| `wss://relay.damus.io`   | Damus main relay        | Free |
| `wss://nos.lol`          | Popular general relay   | Free |
| `wss://relay.primal.net` | Primal's public relay   | Free |

### Interest-Based Discovery

Communities often run their own relays for focused discussions. `wss://nostr.bitcoiner.social` is a long-running example, built around Bitcoin.

Topical relays come and go, so a list printed in a guide goes stale fast. Use the explorer below instead: it checks each relay while you look at it, so you only copy addresses that are actually answering right now.

### Free vs Paid Relays

**Free Relays**

- Open to everyone
- May have rate limits
- Can fill up with spam
- Great for beginners

**Paid Relays**

- Ask for a small Bitcoin payment: some charge once when you join, others monthly
- Prices vary a lot, from a few thousand sats up to around 20,000
- Better performance
- Less spam
- Support relay operators

> **Tip**: Start with free relays. You can always add paid ones later.

### What is NIP-65?

NIP-65 introduces a smarter way to handle relays called the "outbox model."

**Instead of**: You manually connecting to 10+ relays
**You do**: Publish your preferred relay list once, let clients figure it out

### How It Works

Your client publishes one short list, and every relay entry on it is marked as a place you write to, a place you read from, or both. Other people's clients fetch that list before they go looking for you.

- **Write** relays are where you publish. When someone follows you, their client goes to your write relays to pull your posts.
- **Read** relays are where things addressed to you arrive: replies, mentions, notifications. Your client watches them so you see them.

If you get the two backwards, your posts land somewhere nobody thinks to look. Most clients set the markers for you when you edit your relay list in settings, so you rarely touch this by hand.

> The full walkthrough, with what the list actually looks like, is in our [Outbox Model guide](/guides/outbox-model).

### Setting Up Personal Relays

For advanced users, running your own relay gives you:

- **Complete control** over your data
- **A permanent copy** of everything you post, held by you
- **Independence** from any single relay operator's decisions

What it does not give you is reliability or privacy. A relay on your own server is only as dependable as that server, and a large operator usually keeps better uptime than you will. Privacy is a bigger misunderstanding: anything you post still travels out to your other relays and stays publicly readable there. A personal relay only changes where a copy of your posts is kept. Everyone can still read them.

Popular personal relay software:

- [strfry](https://github.com/hoytech/strfry) - Fast C++ implementation
- [nostr-rs-relay](https://github.com/scsibug/nostr-rs-relay) - Rust implementation

> See our [Relay Guide](/guides/relay-guide) for detailed setup instructions.

## Quick Reference

### Common Relay Commands

In most clients, you can:

- **Add relay**: Settings → Relays → Add
- **Remove relay**: Long press/click relay → Remove
- **Check status**: Relay list shows connection status

### Best Practices

1. **Connect to 4-8 relays** for good coverage
2. **Include one "write" relay** where you always post
3. **Check relay uptime** before relying on it
4. **Keep your relay list updated** as the network grows

## Troubleshooting

### "I can't see any posts"

- Check if you're connected to any relays (Settings → Relays)
- Try adding popular relays from the list above
- Wait a moment for content to load

### "My posts disappear"

- Some relays delete old content to save space
- Connect to more relays, so no single one holds your only copy
- Paid relays usually keep posts around much longer, but nothing on Nostr is guaranteed forever: relays prune, change their rules, or close down

### "Relay won't connect"

- Check your internet connection
- Swap the `wss://` at the front of the address for `https://` and open that in your browser: a working relay answers with an info page or a short block of text, a dead one gives you nothing at all
- The relay might be down - try another one
