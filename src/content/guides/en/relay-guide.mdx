## How Relays Work (3 minutes)

<RelayVisualizer client:idle />

<Callout variant="info">
  **Building on Previous Knowledge:** This section briefly reviews the post office 
  analogy from [Relays Demystified](/guides/relays-demystified). If you remember 
  how relays work, feel free to skip to [Types of Relays](#types-of-relays).
</Callout>

### The Simple Explanation

**Relays are servers that store and forward your Nostr messages.**

Think of them like post office boxes:

- You drop a copy of your letter (your post) at several post offices
- Each post office keeps its own copy. They don't forward mail to each other
- Recipients pick it up from a post office you both use

In Nostr:

- **You** write a note (post)
- **Client** sends a copy to each relay you've connected to
- **Relays** store it and serve it to anyone who asks. By default they don't pass it on to other relays
- **Followers** get it from the relays you publish to. Your client announces that list of relays publicly (this is NIP-65), so other people's apps know where to look for you. Older apps that ignore the list only see your posts if you happen to share a relay with them

### Why This Matters

Because your posts sit on several relays at once, one of them going down doesn't take you offline, and nobody can wipe your writing off all of them at the same time. If a relay starts refusing your work, you move to another one and your readers follow. That same openness is why relays differ from each other: some are free, some charge, some filter heavily and some barely at all, and you pick the mix you want to live with.

### Technical Overview [ADVANCED]

Under the hood, relays use WebSocket connections:

```
Client ←→ WebSocket ←→ Relay Server ←→ Database
```

When you post:

1. Client signs the message with your private key
2. Client sends to connected relays via WebSocket
3. Relay validates the signature
4. Relay stores in database
5. Relay broadcasts to subscribers

<Callout variant="info">
  You don't need to understand the technical details. Just know that relays make
  Nostr work without central control.
</Callout>

## Types of Relays (2 minutes)

### Free Public Relays

Volunteers and community members run these, and anyone can post to them. They cost nothing, they're where most people start, and the big ones carry a lot of the network. The trade is that a free relay is only as reliable as the goodwill keeping it up: expect the occasional slow spell or outage.

**Examples:**

- `wss://relay.damus.io` (most popular)
- `wss://nos.lol`
- `wss://relay.snort.social`
- `wss://nostr.mom`

<Callout variant="warning">
  **Skip relay.nostr.band.** It has been offline for a long time now and is not
  expected back. Plenty of guides still list it, which is why a relay that never
  connects is such a common reason a new setup looks broken. Use the relays above.
</Callout>

### Paid Relays

You pay to write to these, usually a fee in bitcoin over Lightning. The money buys an operator who has a reason to keep the machine fast, keep the spam down and answer when something breaks. None of that is necessary for casual posting, and some paid relays cap how much you can store, so treat one as an upgrade you make when a free relay stops being good enough.

**Examples:**

- `wss://nostr.wine` (one-time admission fee)
- Various private relays

### Specialized Relays

Some relays exist for one subject, one region or one app. A smaller room means the people there are likelier to care about what you make, and it costs you nothing to add one alongside the general relays. Just don't rely on a niche relay alone, since only the people who know about it will see you there.

**Examples:**

- Bitcoin-focused relays
- Regional relays (Europe, Asia, etc.)
- Language-specific relays

<Callout variant="success">
  **Start with free public relays.** Add paid ones later if you need better
  performance or want to support the network.
</Callout>

## Choosing Relays (5 minutes)

### Recommended Starter Set

Connect to these four free relays for good coverage:

```
wss://relay.damus.io          (Primary - most popular)
wss://nos.lol                 (Fast, reliable)
wss://relay.snort.social      (Runs behind the Snort client)
wss://nostr.mom               (Smaller, community-run)
```

If you later want a paid relay in the mix, `wss://nostr.wine` is the best-known option. It's covered further down.

<Callout variant="info">
  **A Note on Decentralization:** While the popular relays above are great for
  getting started, relying only on a few large relays concentrates power and
  goes against Nostr's decentralized ethos. As you become more comfortable,
  consider adding smaller, community-run relays and paid relays to support
  network diversity. The goal is a healthy mix of large and small relays, so
  that you're not putting all your eggs in one basket.
</Callout>

### Read Relays and Write Relays

Relay settings usually come in two columns. Write relays hold your own posts, so that's where a new follower's app goes looking for you. Read relays are the ones you watch for replies and mentions coming back. When you save your relay settings, your client publishes this list as a public note that any other app can read, which is how people find your posts without you telling them anything. There's nothing to hand out by hand. [The outbox model guide](/guides/outbox-model) covers the mechanics if you're curious.

### Selection Criteria

**1. Uptime.** A relay that's down half the time is a relay your readers can't reach. nostr.watch tracks uptime if you want to check before adding one.

**2. Location.** A relay physically near you feels faster, so keep one or two close by and a couple further afield for reach.

**3. Content policy.** Some relays filter spam aggressively, some accept nearly everything. Read the operator's stated policy and pick what you can live with.

**4. Speed.** If a relay takes several seconds to connect every time you open your app, it's costing you more than it gives.

**5. The operator.** Someone who answers questions and says publicly what the relay does is worth more than raw specifications.

### Where to Find Relays

- **nostr.watch** - Relay uptime monitor
- **nostr.info** - Relay directory
- **Client recommendations** - Many suggest default relays
- **Community lists** - Shared in Nostr posts

### How Many Relays?

| User Type   | Recommended | Maximum |
| ----------- | ----------- | ------- |
| Beginner    | 3-5         | 10      |
| Active User | 5-10        | 20      |
| Power User  | 10-15       | 30      |
| Bot/Service | 20+         | 50+     |

**More isn't always better:**

- More relays = more bandwidth
- Diminishing returns after 10-15
- Quality over quantity

<Callout variant="warning">
  Don't add 50+ random relays. It wastes bandwidth and slows down your client.
  Stick to 5-10 quality relays.
</Callout>

## Adding Relays (3 minutes)

### Generic Steps (Most Clients)

1. **Open Settings**
   - Usually gear icon or "Settings" menu

2. **Find Relays Section**
   - Look for "Relays" or "Network"

3. **Add New Relay**
   - Click "+" or "Add Relay"
   - Enter WebSocket URL: `wss://relay.example.com`
   - Save

4. **Verify Connection**
   - Should show green dot or "connected"
   - May take a few seconds

### Where the Setting Lives in Each App

Apps move their menus around between releases, so take these as a direction to head in. In **Damus** and **Amethyst**, open Settings and look for "Relays". In **Primal**, it sits under Settings, then Network. In web clients like **Iris** or **Coracle**, it's in the settings menu behind your avatar. If none of that matches what you see, type "relay" into the app's own settings search.

You can practise the whole click-through without touching your real account in the client simulators at [sandstr.app](https://sandstr.app).

### Troubleshooting Connection Issues

**"Failed to connect"**

- Make sure the address starts with `wss://`, not `https://`, `http://` or `ws://`
- Relay might be down
- Try again in a few minutes

**"Timeout"**

- Relay is slow or overloaded
- Try different relay
- Check your internet connection

**No posts loading**

- May need to wait for sync
- Try refreshing
- Check if relay has your followers' posts

## Paid Relays (3 minutes)

### Why Pay?

A paid relay has an operator who can afford to care. That usually shows up as fewer outages, less spam in your replies and someone who actually responds when you report a problem. The moment to consider one is when free relays start letting you down, or when your archive matters enough to you that you want it somewhere with a bill attached.

### Popular Paid Options

**nostr.wine** charges a one-time admission fee in bitcoin over Lightning (around 19,000 sats when this was written, so check the site for today's price). What you get is a fast, well-kept general-purpose relay.

**Private relays** are run for a group: a magazine, a community, a family. Pricing varies and you normally get in by invitation. Worth asking about if you already belong to a circle that has one.

### Setting Up Paid Relays

1. **Choose provider**
2. **Pay** (usually Bitcoin/Lightning)
3. **Receive credentials** (if required)
4. **Add to client** (same as free relays)
5. **Enjoy better performance**

<Callout variant="success">
  **Hybrid approach:** Use 3-5 free relays + 1-2 paid relays. Best of both
  worlds.
</Callout>

## Your Own Relay [ADVANCED] (2 minutes)

Running your own relay means one copy of your work lives on a machine nobody else controls, under whatever rules you set. It also means renting a server (roughly $5 to $20 a month), pointing a domain at it and being comfortable working in a terminal. Most creators never need this, and skipping it costs you nothing: publishing to relays other people run is the normal way to use Nostr.

If you want to try anyway, three relay programs come up again and again. **strfry** is written in C++ and is the fast, lean option. **nostr-rs-relay** is the Rust one, actively developed. **nostream** is TypeScript and has the gentlest setup of the three. Each project keeps installation instructions in its own repository, and those stay current in a way a guide like this can't, so start there rather than from a copied command list.

<Callout variant="warning">
  A relay is a thing you keep running, not a thing you set up once. Disks fill,
  software needs updating, backups need checking. Don't start unless you want
  that job.
</Callout>

## Troubleshooting (3 minutes)

### Common Issues

**Feed is empty**

- Not connected to enough relays
- Relays are down
- Not following enough people

**Fix:**

1. Check relay connections
2. Add more popular relays
3. Follow more people

**Posts not showing up**

- Relay rejected post (rate limit, content policy)
- Client not broadcasting
- Network issues

**Fix:**

1. Check relay policies
2. Try different relay
3. Restart client

**Slow performance**

- Too many relays
- Slow relays in list
- Poor connection

**Fix:**

1. Remove slow relays
2. Stick to 5-10 quality relays
3. Check internet connection

**Can't connect to relay**

- Relay is down
- URL is wrong
- Firewall blocking

**Fix:**

1. Check relay status on nostr.watch
2. Verify URL format (wss://)
3. Try different network

### Monitoring Tools

- **nostr.watch** - Relay uptime and stats
- **nostr.info** - Relay directory and info
- **Client relay status** - Most clients show connection status

### When to Switch Relays

**Switch if:**

- Frequently offline
- Very slow
- Censoring content you care about
- Better alternatives available

**Don't switch just because:**

- One missed post
- Temporary slowdown
- Minor inconvenience
